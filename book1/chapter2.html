Section A — Evolution of Licensing (Q1–10)
Question 1
Which organization led the campaign to regulate the engineering profession in Canada in the late 19th century?
Correct: B. The CSCE held its first general meeting in 1887 and led the campaign to regulate engineering. It later changed its name to the Engineering Institute of Canada (EIC) in 1918.
Question 2
What was the minimum age and experience requirement for admission to the CSCE in its early years?
Correct: C. Applicants to the CSCE were required to be at least 30 years of age with at least 10 years of experience, and had to show responsible charge of work for at least five years.
Question 3
Which country first enacted a law to regulate engineering practice, and in what year?
Correct: A. The United States enacted the first engineering licensing law in 1907, after many instances of gross incompetence were observed in construction.
Question 4
In what year did Ontario finally close its engineering Act, making licensing mandatory rather than voluntary?
Correct: D. Ontario passed its licensing law in 1922 but it was "open." Ontario closed its Act in 1937. (Alberta closed its Act in 1930.)
Question 5
Which was the last Canadian province to enact closed engineering licensing legislation?
Correct: B. Prince Edward Island, in 1955, was the last province to enact closed legislation, making licensing mandatory across all of Canada.
Question 6
A key structural difference between Canadian and U.S. engineering licensing is that in Canada:
Correct: C. In Canada, the profession is self-regulating: each province/territory has passed an Act creating an Association whose governing council is elected by licensed members. In the U.S., state governments appoint licensing boards.
Question 7
Which tragic event is most directly credited with spurring the regulation of geoscientists in Canada, in the same way the Quebec Bridge collapse spurred engineer regulation?
Correct: A. The Bre-X fraud, exposed in 1997, spurred the regulation of geoscientists the way the Quebec Bridge collapse spurred engineer regulation. The Walkerton tragedy reinforced this lesson but came later.
Question 8
In which year did the Canadian Engineering Institute publish the Model Act, and what percentage of members approved it by mail ballot?
Correct: D. In September 1919, the EIC Journal announced that 77% of members had approved the Model Act by mail ballot, after the Act was drafted and published in the EIC Journal.
Question 9
The Walkerton water tragedy in 2000 resulted in seven deaths and more than 2,300 illnesses. Which Ontario legislation was passed shortly thereafter?
Correct: B. Shortly after Walkerton, Ontario passed the Professional Geoscientists Act (2000), Quebec passed the Geologists Act (2001), and Nova Scotia passed the Geoscience Profession Act (2002).
Question 10
In the United Kingdom, engineering is NOT a licensed profession. What title is awarded by British engineering societies to qualified members who join voluntarily?
Correct: C. British engineering societies award the title of Chartered Engineer to members who join voluntarily and meet admission requirements. In the UK, licensing is not compulsory.
Section B — Regulatory Acts & Definitions (Q11–20)
Question 11
How many Canadian provinces or territories regulate engineering and geoscience under a single combined Act?
Correct: A. Eight provinces or territories regulate engineering and geoscience in the same Act. Three provinces (Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia) have separate Acts for engineers and geoscientists.
Question 12
Which two jurisdictions regulate engineering but do NOT yet regulate geoscience?
Correct: D. Prince Edward Island and Yukon regulate engineering but do not yet regulate geoscience, as stated in Table 2.1.
Question 13
According to the Engineers Canada model definition, the practice of engineering concerns the safeguarding of which of the following?
Correct: B. The full Engineers Canada model definition includes the safeguarding of life, health, property, economic interests, the public welfare, and the environment.
Question 14
What is the key calculation that typically determines whether an engineering design protects public life, health, property, or welfare?
Correct: C. The factor of safety — the ratio of load capacity to expected load on a component, system, or structure — is typically the key calculation deciding whether a design protects the public.
Question 15
Under the Alberta definition of engineering, which of the following activities is explicitly included in the practice of engineering?
Correct: A. The Alberta definition explicitly includes teaching engineering at a university as part of the practice of engineering, in addition to the standard technical activities.
Question 16
The Geoscientists Canada model definition states that the practice of geoscience involves advancing knowledge through:
Correct: D. The model definition states: "The practice of geoscience is when a person, through specialized education, training and experience, advances knowledge of the Earth and earth-forming processes through scientific investigation and interpretation."
Question 17
What is the distinction between "regulations" and "bylaws" within a provincial engineering Association?
Correct: B. Regulations clarify the Act or define procedures (admission, professional conduct, disciplinary procedures). Bylaws are rules for running the Association itself, such as election procedures.
Question 18
In Quebec, professional engineering and geoscience Associations are known by a different name. What are they called?
Correct: C. In Quebec, the Associations are called Ordres (e.g., Ordre des ingénieurs du Québec), following the Quebec professional regulatory framework.
Question 19
Which of the following best describes the purpose stated in every provincial or territorial engineering Act?
Correct: A. Every Act has the purpose of protecting the public. The licensing and regulatory framework exists to protect the public welfare, not to serve the interests of the profession itself.
Question 20
A Canadian professional engineering or geoscience licence differs from a U.S. licence in that a Canadian licence confers:
Correct: D. A Canadian licence confers both the right to practise the profession and the right to the title (P.Eng. or P.Geo.). In the United States, licensing only confers the right to use the title.
Section C — Licensing Requirements (Q21–33)
Question 21
How many years of suitable engineering or geoscience experience are required for licensing in most Canadian provinces (excluding Quebec)?
Correct: B. The experience requirement is four years of suitable engineering or geoscience experience. Quebec requires three years of experience.
Question 22
The Professional Practice Examination (PPE) tests applicants on which of the following?
Correct: C. The PPE tests non-technical knowledge of professionalism, ethics, self-regulation, governance of the professions, law, and legal concepts. It does not test technical engineering knowledge.
Question 23
Under the Canadian experience requirements, at least how many months of practical experience must be in a Canadian environment?
Correct: A. All Associations require applicants to obtain 12 months of work experience in a Canadian environment. This usually means working in Canada under a licensed professional.
Question 24
Graduates of programs accredited by the CEAB are exempt from which component of the licensing exam process?
Correct: D. CEAB-accredited graduates are exempt from technical exams, but all applicants must still write the Professional Practice Examination regardless of their academic credentials.
Question 25
For each branch of engineering, approximately how many three-hour technical examinations are there in the Engineers Canada syllabus?
Correct: B. For each branch of engineering, there are about 14 to 18 three-hour technical examinations. Associations normally do not admit applicants to the exam program if the subset assigned exceeds nine exams.
Question 26
What is the maximum pre-graduate work experience credit granted by most Associations toward the experience requirement?
Correct: C. Most Associations grant up to 12 months' credit for work experience obtained before university graduation. The pre-graduate experience should preferably be obtained after the midpoint of the academic program.
Question 27
An applicant holds an engineering degree from a university whose program is accredited by the U.S. ABET. How would Engineers Canada typically treat this degree?
Correct: A. Engineers Canada has a mutual recognition agreement with ABET, recognizing U.S. ABET-accredited degrees as equivalent to Canadian degrees. A Board of Examiners may still overrule if the record appears deficient.
Question 28
The Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) examination is a comprehensive exam that can satisfy confirmatory exam requirements in Alberta and some other provinces. It is administered by:
Correct: D. The FE examination is administered by the U.S. NCEES. It is an eight-hour exam with 120 general questions in the morning session and 60 discipline-specific questions in the afternoon.
Question 29
Which of the following is NOT listed as one of the six basic requirements for obtaining a professional engineering or geoscience licence in Canada?
Correct: B. The six basic requirements are: education, experience, knowledge of local practices, language proficiency, good character, and knowledge of professional practice and ethics. Canadian citizenship is NOT a basic requirement (though some provinces like Alberta request it for full membership).
Question 30
When must an applicant typically write the Professional Practice Examination relative to their work experience?
Correct: C. The PPE is usually written during the second or third year of work experience. It is not necessary to complete all four years before writing it.
Question 31
The "Application of theory" criterion for quality engineering experience requires which type of engagement above all others?
Correct: A. Application of theory — analysis, design and synthesis, testing methods, and implementation — is mandatory and must involve substantial experience. It is one of five quality criteria for engineering experience.
Question 32
Unlike engineering, geoscience degree programs in Canada have no accreditation process. This means that licensing bodies must:
Correct: D. Since no accreditation process exists for geoscience programs, licensing bodies must examine each applicant's undergraduate transcripts and make a course-by-course comparison with the Geoscience Knowledge and Experience Requirements (GKE).
Question 33
Which province is unique in Canada in that it regulates the designation of "Consulting Engineer," and what is the minimum private practice experience required?
Correct: B. Ontario is the only jurisdiction in Canada that regulates the designation of "Consulting Engineer." To qualify, a member must have been continuously engaged for at least two years in private practice and have at least five years of experience since licensing.
Section D — Seals, Discipline & the Code of Ethics (Q34–41)
Question 34
When should a professional engineer apply their professional seal to a document?
Correct: C. The seal must be applied only to final documents. Preliminary documents must be marked "preliminary" or "not for construction" and are NOT sealed. The seal implies intimate knowledge of and control over the document.
Question 35
A professional engineer seals and signs a set of drawings that were prepared entirely by a junior colleague without the engineer's review or supervision. This is most likely to constitute:
Correct: A. A professional who knowingly signs or seals a document not personally prepared (or prepared by assistants under supervision) may be charged with professional misconduct and may also be liable for damages if the misrepresentation results in a loss.
Question 36
What is "professional misconduct" as defined in provincial engineering Acts?
Correct: D. Professional misconduct means negligence, incompetence, or corruption, including a serious infraction of the Association's Code of Ethics. The Association may reprimand, suspend, or expel members found guilty of professional misconduct.
Question 37
The voluntary oath called the "Obligation of the Engineer," first used in 1925, was written by which famous author?
Correct: B. The Obligation of the Engineer is a voluntary oath written by Rudyard Kipling, first used in 1925. Engineers who take the oath wear an iron ring on their working hand.
Question 38
What is the critical difference between the "Obligation of the Engineer" (iron ring ceremony) and the Code of Ethics?
Correct: C. The Obligation is a voluntary commitment to high standards. However, infractions of the Code of Ethics are subject to discipline under the provincial Act. They must not be confused.
Question 39
The Code of Ethics in each provincial Act covers the professional's duties to which of the following groups?
Correct: A. The Code of Ethics includes, in general terms, the duties of the professional to the public, to the employer or client, to fellow professionals, to the profession, and to oneself.
Question 40
When two or more professionals collaborate on different aspects of a project and both seal a document, what must each professional do?
Correct: D. When two or more professionals collaborate, both seals are applied and each professional specifies their area of responsibility in writing next to the seal to delineate accountability.
Question 41
The wearing of an iron ring on the working hand signifies that the engineer has:
Correct: B. Wearing an iron ring does NOT mean the wearer has received a degree or holds a licence. It indicates participation in the ring ceremony and a voluntary oath to maintain high standards in professional work.
Section E — National Bodies, Qualified Persons & Bre-X (Q42–50)
Question 42
Engineers Canada holds the Canadian trademarks for which of the following engineering titles?
Correct: C. Engineers Canada holds the Canadian trademarks for P.Eng., Professional Engineer, Engineer, Engineering, Consulting Engineer, and the French equivalents. This prevents unauthorized use of these titles.
Question 43
The CEAB (Canadian Engineering Accreditation Board) is a committee of Engineers Canada whose primary role is to:
Correct: A. The CEAB evaluates undergraduate engineering programs at Canadian universities, develops academic criteria, arranges accreditation visits, and recommends for or against accreditation.
Question 44
Under National Instrument 43-101 (NI 43-101), who is the only person permitted to publicly disclose scientific or technical information regarding a mineral project?
Correct: D. NI 43-101 states that only a Qualified Person (QP) — as defined by the Canadian Securities Administrators — can disclose scientific or technical information to the public regarding a mineral project.
Question 45
One of the requirements for a Qualified Person (QP) under NI 43-101 is a minimum of how many years of experience in mineral exploration, mine development, or mineral project assessment?
Correct: B. The CSA defines a QP as requiring at least five years of experience in mineral exploration, mine development or operation, or mineral project assessment (or combination thereof) that is relevant to their professional degree or area of practice.
Question 46
In the Bre-X fraud, what was the most critical breach of accepted geological practice that allowed the salting to go undetected?
Correct: C. Drill cores are ordinarily sawn in half along the centreline — one half tested and one half stored for double-checking. Bre-X did not follow this practice, and a single individual controlled all sample testing, allowing salting to proceed undetected.
Question 47
What were the estimated total investor losses due to the Bre-X fraud?
Correct: A. Estimates of the total loss to investors because of the Bre-X fraud run as high as $6 billion. The fraud seriously damaged the Canadian mining industry, making it difficult for legitimate junior mining companies to raise capital.
Question 48
Canada has an estimated 30,000 "brownfields." The restoration of these contaminated properties requires the supervision of a QP and professionals are governed by which legislation?
Correct: D. Professionals in the role of a QP in brownfield restoration are governed by the provincial and territorial Environmental Protection Act. Brownfields are typically sites of former gas stations, toxic dumps, or abandoned mines.
Question 49
Under Canada's Agreement on Internal Trade (AIT), what mobility rights do licensed professional engineers and geoscientists have?
Correct: B. The AIT is federal law enabling any worker qualified for an occupation in any part of Canada to work in that occupation anywhere in Canada. Licensed professionals in good standing can be licensed easily in other jurisdictions.
Question 50
A corporation that provides professional engineering services to the public in most Canadian provinces must obtain:
Correct: C. A corporation must obtain a Permit to Practise (Certificate of Authorization), employ a professional engineer or geoscientist who assumes personal supervisory responsibility, and obtain liability insurance. In Ontario, every entity offering services to the public requires this certificate.
